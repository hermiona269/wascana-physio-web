Our specialty

Pelvic health & incontinence

Bladder and bowel leaking, sudden urgency, the tampon dance in the parking lot, prolapse heaviness, pelvic pain, pain with sex. We assess what's actually happening and treat it like the musculoskeletal issue it is — because it almost always is one.

And no — Kegels aren't automatically the answer. For a lot of women they make things worse. That's exactly why an assessment matters.

  • Pregnancy & postnatalPre & postnatal

    Hip and back pain while pregnant, C‑section and perineal scar recovery, checking your deep core (diastasis) before you return to exercise, and getting back to running and lifting without leaks.

  • Pelvic pain

    Endometriosis-related pain, vulvodynia, tailbone pain, pain with insertion — a plan that starts with listening, not minimizing.

  • Prolapse

    Heaviness, dragging, the "something's coming down" feeling. Conservative management, pessary coaching, and strength that keeps symptoms manageable.

  • Surgery prep & recovery

    Hysterectomy, prolapse repair, C‑section: what to do in the weeks before, and a clear 12‑week path after.

  • Menopause & bone health

    Osteoporosis‑safe strength training, posture and balance work, and fracture prevention — on the windiest, sunniest, vitamin‑D‑lightest prairie there is.

  • Family physiotherapyEveryone else, too

    Backs and necks, sports injuries, arthritis, balance and falls, knee and shoulder rehab — book the whole household, from the teenager's ankle to Grandma's hip.

Your first visit

Here's exactly what will happen

Call or request a time

Tell us the problem in as few words as you like. If we're not the right fit, we'll say so and point you to someone who is.

Assessment visit (45–60 min)

History, movement, and a real conversation. Wear comfortable clothing — there's a change room, and gowns if you'd prefer.

Consent, step by step

For pelvic health, an internal assessment is often the fastest route to answers — but it happens only if you consent, and you can stop at any point. Great care is possible without one.

A plan you can live with

Exercises, habits, a timeline, roughly how many visits you'll need, and what each one costs — before you commit to anything.

Privacy: our women's health rooms are at the back of the clinic with their own waiting corner. Nobody overhears your appointment, and nobody sees your chart. Treatment is with a registered physiotherapist of your choosing where scheduling allows.

Fees & coverage

The plain truth about paying for it

Saskatchewan Health

Private physiotherapy visits aren't covered by provincial health — but you don't need a referral to book. Physiotherapists are direct‑access providers in Saskatchewan.

Hospital‑based outpatient physio is covered, with wait times that can stretch for months. Most of our patients use their extended benefits instead.

Extended benefits

We direct bill most plans, so you pay only your share:

  • Blue Cross SK
  • Canada Life / Great‑West / Sun Life
  • Green Shield · Manulife
  • WCB & SGI claims

A few insurers still require a physician's referral to reimburse — worth a five‑minute check with your plan.

Questions we hear at the desk

Frequently asked, answered plainly

Do I need a doctor's referral?
No. In Saskatchewan you can book directly with a physiotherapist — it's a direct‑access profession. A few insurance plans still ask for a physician's referral before they'll reimburse, so it's worth checking your benefits book before your first visit.
Is this covered by my insurance?
Saskatchewan Health doesn't cover private clinic visits, but most extended‑benefit plans do (typically $50–$150 per visit up to an annual cap). We direct bill Blue Cross SK, Canada Life, Sun Life, Green Shield, Manulife and most others, and we handle WCB and SGI claims.
Will you make me do an internal exam?
Never without your consent, and consent is revisited at every step. Internal assessment is often the fastest way to figure out what's going on with pelvic floor symptoms — but plenty of women improve with external work alone, and that's a legitimate plan. You can stop at any time, and you can ask questions first — do.
What should I bring / wear?
Comfortable clothes you can move in (shorts are easier than jeans), any imaging or surgical reports if you have them, and your insurance card if you plan to use benefits. First visits run 45–60 minutes — build in ten extra minutes for the paperwork.
Can I bring my kids or my partner?
Partners are welcome at women's health appointments — your call entirely. Kids can wait in the front area; tell us when booking and we'll time it around nap schedules if we can help it.
How soon can I be seen?
Usually within one to two weeks. Post‑surgical referrals (hysterectomy, prolapse repair, C‑section) get priority slots — call and say it's post‑op.
Is it too late if this has been happening for years?
No. Our oldest "I should have come in sooner" story in the file is twenty‑two years. The pelvic floor responds to treatment at every age — including after menopause.
